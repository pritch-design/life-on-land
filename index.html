01 / About the project

Small actions can make a difference

Litter affects the appearance of our communities, harms wildlife and can damage natural environments. This project explores the issue of littering in Newport and Cardiff and considers how practical action can help create cleaner local areas.

♧

My project commitment

I plan to spend one hour a day for 15 days, giving a total of 15 hours, cleaning areas within a 1.5-mile radius of my home. My aim is to help keep local areas tidier and inspire others to take greater responsibility for their surroundings.

02 / Preparation

Aims, objectives and planning

01

Improve local areas

Carry out litter-picking sessions in suitable public areas within a 1.5-mile radius of my home.

02

Raise awareness

Use this website to explain why litter is harmful and encourage responsible waste disposal.

03

Inspire action

Show how individual effort can contribute to cleaner communities in Newport and Cardiff.

03 / Staying safe

Risk assessment

I will assess each location before starting and avoid anything that could put me or others at risk.

Possible hazardPrecaution
Broken glass or sharp objectsWear suitable gloves; do not handle sharp items directly. Leave dangerous objects and report them to the appropriate authority.
Traffic and roadsUse pavements and safe crossing points. Stay away from roadsides and never enter the carriageway to collect litter.
Unknown or hazardous wasteDo not touch chemicals, needles, medical waste or suspicious items. Move away and report them.
Weather and uneven groundCheck the forecast, wear suitable footwear and clothing, and stop if conditions become unsafe.
Manual handling and hygieneAvoid lifting heavy objects, wash hands thoroughly afterwards and dispose of collected waste responsibly.

Safety first: I will not attempt to remove hazardous waste. If I am unsure whether an item is safe to handle, I will leave it and report it.

05 / Research review

What my research tells me

The research suggests that littering is not just an issue of appearance. It can affect wildlife, damage natural environments and reduce the quality of public spaces. The sources also show that litter and fly-tipping create costs for local authorities, which must spend time and money clearing waste.

Using a range of sources helps me consider the issue from different perspectives. Welsh Government information provides statistics about fly-tipping, news reports show how litter affects particular places and communities, and environmental organisations explain the consequences for wildlife and habitats.

Some figures refer to different locations, surveys or types of waste, so they should not automatically be treated as directly comparable. I will use this research to inform my project and then collect my own observations during the practical work.
