01 / THE PROJECT

My project.

Why this issue matters to me, what I plan to do and what I hope to achieve.

Litter is a persistent problem in Newport and Cardiff.

I have chosen this topic because litter affects the overall wellbeing of communities. I believe it is an issue that needs to be addressed. For my project, I will spend 15 hours collecting and disposing of litter in my local area. I plan to spend one hour a day over 15 days, cleaning an area within a one-and-a-half-mile radius of my home.

My overall aim is to help keep Newport and Cardiff tidier and, hopefully, encourage others to take similar action.

Welsh Government (2026)

  • Over 48,000 fly tipping incidents were reported in Wales between 2024 and 2025
  • The Vale of Glamorgan reported a 100% annual increase in fly tipping incidents
  • Newport reported a 10% annual decrease in fly tipping incidents
  • Over 71% of incidents involved household waste
  • Of the over 27,000 enforcement actions, 22,924 were investigations (83%)
  • Of the 70 prosecution outcomes, 80% resulted in a fine

Research review.

Research, evidence and personal perspective.

CAUSEEFFECT

My research shows that litter is one of the most prominent issues affecting the local area. Keeping the environment clean is important because litter can harm natural surroundings and wildlife.

The resources I have studied broadly support my view that littering is a serious issue. For example, Resource 3, a BBC article, describes how people in the Lake District have come to expect litter, suggesting that it is becoming normalised. This is deeply concerning. My research review notes that 93% of people in the Lake District had experienced littering. This suggests that future generations could come to see this behaviour as normal. The RSPB article explains that animals may eat litter, including sharp objects, when searching for food. This can injure wildlife and may even lead to premature death. Resource 2 states that ‘People often fly tip in communities that they don't live in’. This behaviour can damage the appearance and wellbeing of communities. Similarly, the CIEH article describes litter as ‘a huge societal problem’, with potential consequences for the environment and wildlife.

I recognise that some people may feel that the seriousness of littering is sometimes exaggerated. However, I believe there are good reasons for concern, particularly when litter is being left in someone's own community. There may be bias in how people discuss this issue, but I believe the concern is justified. Littering is often dismissed as a minor problem, yet it can have serious consequences and should be addressed. Fines can act as a deterrent, although identifying and taking action against offenders can be difficult. Resource 4 highlights the negative effects litter can have on human health, while Resource 5 explains that animals often eat litter. I understand that some people may believe littering causes little harm, but I believe people need to be properly informed about the threats it poses to the environment.

The sources I have chosen are relevant and have useful credibility. Resource 1 is published by the Welsh Government and provides information about fly-tipping in Wales, making it particularly relevant to my local area. Its publication by the Welsh Government also makes it a credible source for information about this issue.
